    How the radar prices a launch

    implied value = curve(TVL) × spike discount × 0.60

    curve(TVL) = exp(6.9646 + 0.5223 × ln TVL) · spike discount = exp(−2·idio / 100)

    The size curve — fitted, not assumed

    Fitted at run time by ordinary least squares on log market cap against log TVL over the snapshot's 128 tokenized peers (a positive market cap and a positive TVL): intercept 6.9646, slope 0.5223, R² 0.194, residual RMSE 2.0771 in log space. The market pays less per TVL dollar the bigger a protocol is, so the multiple falls with size:

    A flat multiple would charge the $2.76B protocol the same per-dollar price as the $5M one. The curve charges it twenty times less — that is what the peers' own record says, and it is why the board's values run $0.7M–$48.2M instead of tracking TVL dollar for dollar.

    About that last row: the free regression's growth slope is −6.24 — a protocol whose TVL spiked is worth much less per TVL dollar. Applied raw it would rank a protocol down 73% in a week fourth on the board, and a shrinking protocol at #3, on a mean-reversion artifact. Radar-6 applies the direction at one-third strength (k = −2, spanning 0.30–1.82), which beats k = −1 on both tests. The k-grid is monotone — stronger keeps winning held-out (k = −3: 2.0717 / 2.0782; k = −6: 2.0606 / 2.0409) — so no blind test pins k, and k = −2 is the stated trade-off, not a fitted value: at k = −2 the board's top five are unchanged from k = −1, while stronger k starts promoting crashed protocols into the top 10 on the spike alone. The clearest case is b14g, which enters the top 10 at #8: it fell 28.5% in a week against −12.2% for its Restaking category (idio −16.3), so its spike discount at k = −2 is 1.39×, lifting a $173.9M TVL to $17.7M implied. That is the peer-validated mean-reversion the model is built on, stated with its numbers rather than hidden behind them. A launch-odds term was tested too and dropped: a logistic fit on age and TVL predicts which protocols have a token at leave-one-out log-loss 0.6842 against the base rate's 0.6884 (AUC 0.608) — barely better than guessing — and its age coefficient is positive: the share with a token RISES with age, 26% under six months to 79% past three years. No "odds fall with age" claim is made, and no odds term is applied.

    1. The curve is the price. The protocol's TVL through the fitted log-log curve is the size of the thing being priced. Because the curve is concave, a protocol ten times bigger is worth about three times more, not ten times — and the board's order is the curve's order, not TVL's.
    2. A TVL spike is a discount, not a premium. The protocol's seven-day TVL change minus its category's median change that week — the move that is the protocol's own, not the market's — enters as exp(−2·idio / 100), clamped to −30%…+60%. A protocol up 14% against its category is discounted to 0.76×; one down 30% is lifted to 1.82×. In this snapshot the median protocol fell 8.2% in a week, so the category baseline is what keeps market beta out.
    3. Discount. The base case discounts 40% for execution risk. The board shows the range too: bear at a 50% discount, bull at 30%. A single number would pretend to be exact; the range is honest about what a launch is worth.
    4. Coverage. Only protocols with no token on record, and none of the wrapped, staked or bridged products of tokens that already trade — they are not launches. So are exchange-named products, versioned pools and CEXes. The watchlist is the floor, never the ceiling: 20 rankable protocols above it are ranked too, and the biggest of them sits at #2.
    5. Farm plans. Every row carries a seven-step plan built from its own record — capital sized by TVL ($50–$250), gas priced per chain, a weekly cadence that reads the protocol's own seven-day move (wait out a spike over +20, enter on a fall under −15), a hold of 30–60 days by age, a second chain where the protocol has one, and an exit step — plus a one-line farm verdict (priority, capital, gas, hold) so two rows can be compared without opening either plan.
    6. What the model does not do. It does not reward age, category leadership or chain count: each was tested held-out and lost both tests, so each is left out and the table above says so. The 0.60 discount stays as an execution-risk judgment, not a fitted value: the peers' record calibrates the curve, not the launch risk — the leave-one-out residuals average −0.0143, so the constant that best predicts existing tokens' market caps is about 0.99, and fitting the discount to the peers would erase the discount the board exists to carry. The snapshot carries no fees or revenue fields, so no revenue term is invented: the market's own mcap/TVL curve is the multiple the snapshot supports.

    No name is special-cased: the same fit and the same two factors score every row, and the detail view shows each row's curve multiple, its spike discount and its range, so any rank can be checked by hand from the snapshot.
